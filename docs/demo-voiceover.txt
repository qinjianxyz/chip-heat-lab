Chip Heat Lab is a clean room open source demo of engineering simulation for chip design. It is not a production validation workflow. It is a narrow design review cockpit for one stylized AI accelerator floorplan.

The first screen shows the value loop. The baseline clustered SRAM design fails three simplified checks: steady thermal peak, transient thermal dose, and a power delivery droop proxy. Rust owns the benchmark JSON behind those checks.

The cockpit does not just draw a heatmap. It compares candidate interventions and ranks them against explicit demo constraints. In the checked in review, spread SRAM is the lowest cost passing candidate.

The recommended field is still computed by the Rust solver. The peak estimate drops from seventy one point one Celsius to sixty eight point two Celsius. The transient dose drops from thirteen point five to four point three Celsius seconds. The droop proxy drops from sixty seven point five to fifty point one millivolts.

That is the hardware team workflow we are showing: do not argue about a pretty picture; ask what failed, what intervention passed, what it costs, and what assumption explains the result.

The native macOS app runs the same Rust command line backend through JSON. The Vercel site replays exported Rust artifacts and benchmark outputs; the browser is not a second solver.

GBrain is represented by the importable markdown knowledge base for assumptions, references, explanations, and claim boundaries. GStack is represented by the scope lock, engineering review, QA plan, ship checklist, and retro artifacts.

Chip Heat Lab is useful because it turns an early chip floorplan tradeoff into an inspectable decision artifact: one design, three simplified checks, ranked interventions, tests, benchmarks, and explicit non claims.
